What each pack is

Editorial
Type pairing. A heavy serif for numbers and names with a small-caps sans for labels (drawn here with stand-in fonts; the shipped pack bundles an open-licence face). Shapes. Hairlines, ruled bars and dot ledgers, with square-cut charts. Motion. Slow wipes, and numerals that roll like a ticker. Background. Flat paper, ink or one spot colour taken from the top album.

Tide
Type pairing. Inter, heavy for names and numerals. Shapes. Solid layered waves. The cover is the sun, months become a swell, the genre mix a flowing stream and the hours a tide ring. Motion. The waves drift sideways at 8 to 14 s a cycle, each layer at its own speed, and the water level eases between cards. Background. A different mood on each card (sunset, night, sea) from the palette.

Bloom
Type pairing. Inter, heavy and dark on a warm ground. Shapes. Big soft colour fields, petals for the hours and a living blob for the personality. Motion. The fields breathe by a few percent and the petals open one after another. Background. Four saturated colours from the top covers on cream.

Glass
Type pairing. The system font on Apple devices and Inter elsewhere, in bold and regular. Shapes. Lenses and bubbles. Bubble size follows plays. Motion. Parallax on the glow and a slow lens drift. On Apple devices the lens is Liquid Glass; elsewhere it is a frosted tonal layer. Background. Blurred glows made from the top covers.

How the 2026 palette was made

Each pack takes the clusters it needs, shades them for its ground and checks text contrast against them.

1. Pack. Picked from the shipped packs by a seed made from the profile, the year and the pack version, and never the same pack as last year (never either of the last two once there are six or more).

2. Palette. Built from that year's top three albums with the sampling on board 04, then fitted to the pack as shown above. The lead hue must sit at least 60° from last year's, or the next best colour cluster is used.

3. Layout. The seed also picks one of three or four layouts for every beat and places the shapes, so the same pack looks different from one person to the next and from one year to the next. Reopening it, or opening it on another device, gives the identical result.

4. The data is the artwork. Each pack draws the person's own numbers in its own way: Editorial a ruled bar chart and an hour grid, Tide a swell, a stream and a tide ring, Bloom petals and a blob, Glass bubbles sized by plays and a lens ring.

5. Year line. A sentence built from the top traits ("Late nights, loud choruses") opens the story and titles every share card.

6. Repeat packs still change. The 2030 card uses the Editorial pack again with a new palette and a different layout. After five years every pack has come round and none of them looks the same twice.

7. Devices. The recap data pins the pack. It is chosen from the packs that every device the profile used in the last 90 days has. An older app uses the pack's named fallback and says "Shown in a simpler look on this device".

8. New packs. They arrive with app updates, because the store rules forbid downloaded code (plan section 19). Plan on one or two per release. Held back for later releases: Contour (flowing map lines), Aura (mesh gradient with glass orbs), Neon Grid and Paper Collage.

9. Pack contract. Every pack draws every beat type, passes a 4.5:1 text contrast check, and passes snapshot tests on extreme histories: one artist, 5,000 artists, very light and very dark art, very long names, non-Latin and right-to-left scripts, and 100 plays against 100,000.
