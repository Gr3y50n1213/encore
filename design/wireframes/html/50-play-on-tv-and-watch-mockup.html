Play on a TV, and on your watch

Two roadmap items the owner asked for on 24 November 2026. A TV joins "Play on" beside your other devices: the TV plays the music and shows its own big-screen Encore, and the phone becomes the remote. Watches get Now Playing, the sung lyric line, Like and your mixes. Android and Wear OS are shown; the Apple versions follow in Apple's own style.

How it works

  1. Which TVs. The most common come first: Google Cast, then Roku, then plain DLNA. On Roku, an Encore channel is installed through Roku's developer mode and started over the local network. DLNA is a fallback that plays the music without Encore's screen. An Android TV or projector with the Encore TV app shows up in the list automatically.
  2. Privacy. The TV gets a short-lived pass that can only play the queue it was sent, never your sign-in. The pass ends when you take the music back or stop. Nothing about your library or history is stored on the TV.
  3. Watches. One Wear OS app covers the Samsung Galaxy Watch 4 and later, the Pixel Watch and other Wear OS watches. Later, songs could be downloaded to the watch for runs without the phone. Fitbit OS watches can't take new apps: Google stops third-party apps on them on 28 November 2026.
  4. Nothing else changes. Play on keeps its sheet, and Now Playing keeps its look apart from the band. The TV screen reuses mock-up 45.

Questions for the owner

  • Do Play on, the TV screen and the watch apps look right? (Recommended: yes, as drawn.)
  • Google Cast needs Google's Cast library, which is closed source, so an F-Droid build would leave Cast out. Cast also needs a receiver registered with Google, which costs a one-time US$5, and an https page for the receiver, which the companion server can serve. (Recommended: yes, Cast in the GitHub build.)
  • The Wear OS app's link to the phone needs Google Play services (the Wearable Data Layer library). (Recommended: yes, as Waypoint's watch app does.)
  • Pebble: build the watchapp with Core Devices' current SDK, after I've checked that SDK. (Recommended: yes.)
